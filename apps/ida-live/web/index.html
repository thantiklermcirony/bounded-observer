        Brain rhythms are smooth and shared by neighbouring sensors; muscle comes in spiky bursts on one sensor; electrical interference is smooth and shared by every sensor. Spiky is the kurtosis of 30–95 Hz (3 = smooth noise, far above 3 = sharp interruptions). HF shared and LF shared are each sensor's average correlation with the others above 30 Hz and at 1–20 Hz. 1/f is how steeply its 2–20 Hz power falls (brain > 0.3). Hum ↑ is how far its mains hum has risen above its own last minute: when the high frequencies rise with the hum, the sensor has loosened (loose); when they rise without it, that is muscle. eyes is a forehead burst that came with a blink or eye movement.

        The Murray Reality Equation test: does a random-bit stream switch more often when your information rate Φ is higher? IDA Live counts a random-number device's bits in 100 ms bins, all the time, and tags every bin with what you were doing and your Φ (context-tree entropy of your forehead EEG). Automation (nothing connected) sets the baseline; focused attention is any recording with the headband. The analysis follows the paper's plan exactly, with its three falsifiers.
