# What IDA Live is meant to make you feel, and how we know it is working (v0.6)

## The aim, made measurable
"Maximising the mind" is split into four abilities, and each has a number the app records:

| Ability | What it feels like | How it is measured |
|---|---|---|
| **Steer**: move your own state on purpose | "that was me" (agency) | real rounds above the sealed replay, in the signal and in your ratings |
| **Hold**: stay there without strain | absorption, effortlessness (flow) | time in the corridor with muscle effort at or below your baseline; little "semantic work" |
| **Return**: come back after a shock | surprise, then relief and confidence | recovery time after each knock; its trend across sessions |
| **Widen**: open or narrow attention at will | spaciousness, awe | the attention map's narrow/broad signature, and Night Sky |

Outside the levels, the outcome that matters is how you are: a mood grid before and after each
level (pleasant–unpleasant by calm–energised, Russell's affect grid). Over weeks, that and the
four numbers are the evidence, not any one good round.

## Why it should feel good: the reward is musical resolution
Neurofeedback is operant learning: a state is learned when it is followed, immediately and
reliably, by something rewarding. The strongest natural reward the ear knows is musical
expectation being fulfilled: tension that resolves. Striatal dopamine rises in anticipation
of a musical peak and at the peak itself (Salimpoor et al. 2011), and the pleasure of a
musical moment depends on predictability and surprise together, not either alone (Cheung et al.
2019; Gold et al. 2019).

So each level's music is written to be *unfinished* until your brain finishes it:
* **Off target** the harmony hangs on suspended and dominant chords that want to resolve. It is
  never ugly; it is waiting.
* **In the corridor** the progression is allowed to cadence home on the downbeat. That
  landing, earned and on time, is the reward.
* **Climbing a tier** is built like a peak moment in a film score: a swell and a riser in the
  bar before (the anticipation), then a full cadence and a new instrument entering on the
  downbeat (the moments that most reliably give people chills: a new voice, a crescendo, an
  unexpected harmony; Sloboda 1991, Grewe et al. 2007).
* **Straining** makes the music tighten and thin out; letting go makes it bloom again. Your
  data showed that effort shows up as muscle and pulls the signal the wrong way, so release
  itself is rewarded.

## Should it scare you, surprise you, or what?
**It should surprise you, never frighten you, and then reward your return.** In predictive
terms a knock is a prediction error; learning happens there. Pleasure is highest when a
surprise lands in a predictable setting, or when predictability comes back after uncertainty
(Cheung et al. 2019). The chamber uses both:
* When you are steady and the music is predictable, it adds well-placed surprises: knocks,
  a new voice, an unexpected chord.
* When things are uncertain (you have been knocked), it offers the most predictable thing it has:
  the way home. After each knock the harmony lands somewhere distant and moves one step back
  towards home for every bar you spend in the corridor. When you have recovered, the level's
  theme returns. That is the IDA return, heard.

## How it fits the TAO programme
Sound is an actuator for the same world vector as the picture, and each mapping follows the
meaning of the variable's chart:

| World variable (TAO chart) | Music |
|---|---|
| q clarity (saturating growth) | distance → presence: a far, muffled, reverberant hall opening to a near, bright one |
| f flow membership (logistic distance) | harmonic tension → resolution; cadences are allowed only in the corridor |
| mode / tier (hysteretic modes) | orchestration: one new layer per tier, changing only on bar lines (TAO's blended mode switch) |
| h rhythm (multiplicative gate) | the breath: arpeggios rise on the inhale and fall on the exhale; a defined pulse only when breath is locked AND the signal is steady AND confident |
| n novelty (independent threat) | ornament density: grace notes, sparkles, variations |
| p perturbation (independent threat) | the sting and the displaced harmony, then the road home |
| l load (inhibitory suppression) | the music narrows and quietens (TAO's density reduction for load) |
| a arousal (log-odds corridor) | tempo and weight, held inside a corridor |

The Monk tier is asymptotic, like the boundary of a bounded interval: the theme sounds in full,
but the chamber never "finishes".

## Is it fun?
Each level is a piece you complete. Streaks, returns and tier peaks are audible events; the end
card shows your best moments; your personal bests persist. You can mark a chill (press C) the
moment you feel a rush; the report checks whether your chills fall on the peaks the music was
built for, more than chance.

## What would show it is not working
* Real and replay rounds rated and measured the same, across many sessions.
* Peaks reached only with muscle effort above baseline (strain, not skill).
* Chills no more likely at designed peaks than at random times.
* Mood after no better than before, over weeks.
