# Programme review and research direction — 3 October 2026

A full read of the programme across its repositories, and a decision about where to go next.
Written as the programme's lead scientist would write it: what holds, what does not, why the
tests so far have not discriminated, and the few experiments that could.

Scope read: `bounded-observer` (capstone, docs, toolkit, registry, experiments, IDA Live),
`empirical-observatory`, `empirical-architecture`, `boundedness-atlas`, `ida-stateatlas`, the
profile repository, the four psychiatry documents and the olanzapine submission, the FRBM
aluminium paper, and the five forks. The forks are upstream code; the only programme commit
in them is a publication workflow for a Ray bug fix, which is sound engineering and no
evidence for or against the theory.

Every claim below carries a tag. "Checked here" means I re-ran or re-derived it in this
review. "Audit" means a careful reader in this review reported it and I have not re-run it.

---

## 1. What the programme is, in one paragraph

An observer with finite access, inside the world it measures, must decide what to keep
(predictive state, Theorem 10), which interventions act the same way from every state (the
action gate, A.3), and how kept quantities combine. If a bounded scalar's changes combine
continuously, associatively, strictly monotonically and with a resting state (C1–C4), there is
an additive coordinate, the rapidity, in which the bound sits at infinity (Theorems 1–2, the
UHL). Projectivity, earned separately from a two-channel linear world read through a ratio
(Theorem 6), then fixes the law by its horizon count: two horizons give Einstein/logit
(Theorem 7); one gives the Hamacher dial with Bliss and Loewe on it (Theorem 8). In two or
more dimensions, the observer's channel cone decides the geometry (Theorem 25), and a failure
of associativity is kept as holonomy (Theorem 26, Proposition 8).

## 2. What holds

**The mathematics is correct where checked, and honestly cited.** [P] for the classical core.
- Checked by audit: A.1, A.2, A.4–A.8, A.10, A.15–A.20; Theorem 8 algebra; Theorem 26
  commutator 0.069; gyration −0.60035 equal to the area of (0, a, a⊕b); Proposition 3
  exponents 1, 2, 1+1/(a+2), and about 3 for e^(−1/u); the max-ent bound Var ≤ L(s_max−m)
  never exceeded on 2,000 random measures.
- The capstone itself credits Aczél, Hölder, Luce–Marley, Hamacher, Crutchfield–Young,
  Shalizi–Crutchfield and Littman–Sutton–Singh. Nothing here claims priority it has not earned.

**What is plausibly new, and modest.** [D]
1. The ordering discipline: access, then state, then action gate, then chart, then geometry,
   then prediction. In particular, keeping the action gate separate from statehood.
2. Proposition 3's continuum of boundary exponents and its cost-of-approach bound.
3. Placing the classical drug laws (Hill slope 1) on one Hamacher dial, with a mechanism
   reading for each landmark.
4. Using the Lie bracket fg′−gf′ (Proposition 6) and the cross-ratio (Theorem 6) as tests run
   *before* any chart is fitted.
5. Proposition 8, *if* it survives independent checking. It is the only substantive theorem
   claimed as new, and its proof is not in this repository.

**The record of failures is the programme's best asset.** Kept, frozen, with data:
H1 (refuted), Recovery Lab, Virtual Cell flights 01 and 02, the paired transfer audit, Active
Context, two-family control, TAO vs PI, the Chlamydomonas history feature. Few research
programmes keep this.

## 3. What does not hold yet

**No empirical test anywhere has shown a framework-specific advantage.** [P] as a statement
about the record. One refutation (H1). One replication (H2) that is mainly a replication of
Engels (2000), Deeks (2002) and Zhao (2022), as the capstone says. Eight frozen observatory
tests, all lost to conventional baselines or to their own gates. Everything else is proposed,
not run.

**The horizon cannot be refuted as currently stated.** Checked here. C3 requires strict
monotonicity on the whole interval I. If I contained its end β, then β⊕a > β for a > e, which
is impossible. So the ends are excluded by the premises, and "no finite sequence reaches the
end" is part of the input. The §13.5 refuter "a horizon is reached with every condition intact"
(`papers/capstone/04_test.md:69`) can therefore never fire. The falsifiable content is
Proposition 9: measure γ independently, and predict arrival exactly when γ < 1.

**The hyperbolic content comes from projectivity, not boundedness.** [P], and the capstone
says so (§2.4). Empirical claims that something is "hyperbolic" must therefore test projectivity
first. That is the cross-ratio test. The toolkit has no cross-ratio function.

## 4. Why the tests have not discriminated: three diagnoses

### D1. The tests were run where all the laws agree

Any two dial laws resting at 0 with unit slope at rest differ, to leading order, by
(α₁ − α₂)·a·b. Expand Theorem 8: a⊕b = a + b − (1+α)ab + O(3). [P]

So the laws can only be told apart where the product of the two effects is large, which means
near the bound. Near rest, every lawful composition is flat addition. The capstone says the same in
principle (§14: "everyday experience happens near rest, where flat and rapidity descriptions
agree"). What is new here is applying it to the design of the tests.

The record fits this.
- **H2, checked here.** For rare events −log(1−p) ≈ p, so the Bliss chart (one of the
  programme's own rapidity charts) is almost as heterogeneous as the flat risk difference.
  Median I²: Bliss 0.497 vs RD 0.522 (graves2010: 0.81285 vs 0.81299). 16 of the 19
  meta-analyses have maximum baseline risk below 0.45. Spearman ρ between maximum baseline risk and
  the RD-minus-Bliss gap: 0.08, p = 0.79.
- **H1, audit.** Dial RMSE 0.059 against Bliss 0.065 and Loewe 0.066, near the single-well
  noise floor. α hit a bound in 65% of blocks.

[H] **H1 and H2 lacked the power to separate the charts they compared.**
- *Test:* on the actual DECREASE dose grid, compute the predicted gap between the dial
  laws being compared, and set it against the replicate standard error.
- *Loss condition:* if the median predicted inter-law gap across the 210 blocks is at least 3
  times the replicate SE, lack of power does not explain H1, and H1 stands as evidence against
  the mechanism reading of the dial.

**Result (T1, first pass, narrowed after review).** The stated loss condition was **not met** per
cell: the median predicted Bliss–Loewe gap (0.0102) is 0.50 of a single-agent Hill-residual noise
proxy (0.0198) and 0.14 of every law's misfit (0.065). This is weaker than first written:
- the proxy mixes noise with model error;
- per-cell gaps can aggregate across cells;
- only α = 0 vs α = 1 was compared;
- the power of H1's actual ordinal test was not computed.

A full audit is in progress: all dial positions including Einstein (α = −1), conventional rivals,
independent noise estimates, calibration uncertainty, dependence, and simulated power of the H1
statistic. **H1 remains refuted as pre-registered.** No outcome of this audit is evidence for the
framework.

**Design rule that follows.** No future chart test runs unless a power check shows that the
predicted gap between the rival laws exceeds 3 SE in a pre-stated fraction of cells. This
check should be a toolkit function (`bounded.design`) run before every freeze.

### D2. The history tests bet against the programme's own theorem

Theorem 10 says: if the present is a sufficient state, history adds nothing. Four frozen
tests asked whether history or context adds prediction to an already rich present (Recovery
Lab, Virtual Cell flights 01 and 02, the Chlamydomonas history feature). All found about nothing.

[H] **Those nulls mostly show that the conventional present was already near-sufficient,
not that the framework failed.**
- *Test:* for each of the four, the gain from history should shrink as the richness of the
  present grows (more present covariates, smaller history gain).
- *Loss condition:* history gain does not fall, or rises, as present covariates are added.

The discovery is not in confirming sufficiency. It is in finding where the present is
**not** sufficient (Proposition 1, Case I), and naming the missing coordinate. Chlamydomonas F01
is an example: equal total light, 1 vs 8 descendants by schedule. Total light is not a state.
The published ODE already knows why, so it is not new. But this is the right *kind* of target.

### D3. Too many fronts

There are 39 atlas rows, 41 legacy families, and four different "current focuses" dated 8–15
September: the upstream PRs and AI benchmark, the biological observer loop, IDA, and the six
gates. A programme with no wins yet cannot afford that spread. It needs one flagship test that
can win, run properly.

## 5. Direction

**Stop testing the law. Test its conditions, where they bite.** The UHL is a theorem and
cannot be discovered or refuted. What is open, empirical and largely unmeasured is *which real
bounded quantities satisfy C2 (grouping) and C3 (strictness) near their bounds*, and which pass
the action gate. That is the programme's own territory, and nobody else is working in it.

The work is in four stages, in order of cost.

### Stage 1 — compute-only tests (weeks)

**T1. Power audit of H1 and H2** (D1 above). **First pass done; narrowed after review; full audit in progress**
(`experiments/t1-power-audit/`). H1 stays refuted as pre-registered.

**T2. The nearest-horizon rule.** [H]

H2's win was carried by log RR, not by the Bliss chart. log RR is the rapidity of the
one-horizon chart whose horizon sits at p = 0, and these low-risk trials sit next to that
bound. Read in the programme's own terms:

> Among one-horizon charts, the one whose horizon is nearest the trials' baseline risk is the
> most consistent across trials. Equivalently: the log risk ratio of the *less common* outcome.

- *Prediction:* when median control risk is below 0.5, log RR(event) is more homogeneous than
  log RR(non-event) = the Bliss chart. Above 0.5, the reverse. Logit, with two horizons, is
  never far from the better one.
- *Data:* Cochrane-scale binary meta-analyses spanning high baseline risk. The local `metadat`
  collection cannot test it: all 16 binary sets have median control risk ≤ 0.46 (checked here).
- *Loss condition:* in meta-analyses with median control risk above 0.6, log RR(event) is at
  least as homogeneous as log RR(non-event) in half or more.
- *Prior art:* Deeks (2002) discusses the choice between the risk ratio of the event and of the
  non-event. Read it before claiming anything. If the rule is already there, a pass is a
  replication with a mechanism, not a discovery.

**T3. Grouping near the bound: the flagship.** [H]

The first direct test of C2 on a bounded biological quantity, in the region where laws differ.

- *Data:* public dose–response measurements of drug triples alongside their pairs. Candidate
  sources I know of but have not opened in this review: Zimmer et al. (2016, PNAS) and Tekin
  et al. (2018). Confirm their content and licences before freezing.
- *Design:* fit each pair's dial α from pairs only. Predict each triple under every grouping,
  (a⊕b)⊕c and a⊕(b⊕c). Run the D1 power check first. Restrict scoring to cells where the
  predicted gap between groupings, or between the dial and the best rival, exceeds 3 SE.
- *Prediction:* where a single pairwise law per pair exists, triples are predicted within
  noise and grouping does not matter.
- *Loss condition:* in the power-qualified cells, triples deviate from every pair-composed
  prediction by more than 3 SE in over 20% of cells. That would mean C2 fails, or the
  pairwise state is not sufficient (Case I).
- *Rival:* a flexible pairwise model with no dial structure (e.g. Zimmer's own). The dial
  must at least match it on held-out triples, with fewer parameters.

Why this one: it uses the programme's most developed charts, public data, existing code, and
the H1 experience. Either outcome is informative. A pass is the first measured C2 in biology
near a bound. A failure locates a Case I.

**T4. Independent check of Proposition 8.** [D] until checked. Mathematics, no data.

Search numerically for bounded, O(2)-equivariant, gyro-associative laws on the disk whose
reassociation defects are isometric but not hyperbolic. This attacks the open SO(2), n = 2
case directly.
- *Loss condition:* such a law exists.
- If none is found and a specialist confirms the proof, this is the programme's one new
  theorem. That is worth more than any number of re-readings.

**T5. Theorem 25 item 3.** [H] as stated.

The affine-invariant geometry of the positive-definite cone is a classical symmetric space
with non-positive, non-constant curvature (see Bhatia, *Positive Definite Matrices*, 2007; I
have not reopened the exact theorem in this review). If that settles item 3, re-tag it as [P]
with the citation, and spend no experimental effort on it.

### Stage 2 — one experiment (months, with a collaborator)

Whichever of T2 or T3 passes, take it to new data designed for it. There are two candidates
already on the register, with their designs written:

- **L-08.** The split-dose reversal, 6+12 Gy against 12+6 Gy, on clonogenic survival. This is
  an order test on a quantity with a horizon at zero survival.
- **L-09.** The rescue-window matrix: 5 depths × 7 durations × 3 repeats, with held-out depths.

Adopt the stricter loss condition from the cancer manuscript's own §5.4 for L-09: beat
cumulative exposure T = K/F and a free power law on held-out depths. The current atlas
condition, "the exponent leaves (½, 1)", is close to automatic inside the model's regime.

### What to stop or set aside

- **The "Universal Hyperbolic Law" name for the one-dimensional result.** It selects no
  curvature (§2.4). Keep the name only if the next sentence always says so.
  `empirical-architecture` already uses "UHL" for the narrower projective law. The two
  repositories should agree.
- **The FRBM aluminium paper's uniqueness claim.** It rests on "Aczél gives a unique artanh",
  which the capstone withdraws (§2.4). Its √[Al] threshold prediction is not derived. Plain
  competition for superoxide, k_Al[Al] against k_SOD[SOD], gives a *linear* threshold.
  [D], audit; check before any resubmission.
- **Historical physics claims** (Yang–Mills, "Theory of Everything", microtubules) are rightly
  in `history/`. Keep them out of any current claim.

## 6. The psychiatry documents: urgent, separate from the theory

These sit outside the UHL (atlas X-P1, X-P2), and that placement is correct. The audit
compared their figures with published abstracts and with its own knowledge of the literature.
It did not open the full texts.

The olanzapine submission is meant for formal use. **Before it is used, fix:**
- **The PANSS "overstatement" argument runs backwards.** Correcting for the 30-point floor
  makes a 22.5-point drop from 90 *larger* (22.5/60 = 37.5%), not smaller.
- **Pottegård 2018 is reported as a 52% increase.** The paper reports OR 1.18 for long-term
  use and no clinically important association.
- **The Lilly 2009 plea is described as a criminal fraud conviction.** It was a misdemeanour
  plea for off-label promotion.

Across Parts I–V, these figures need re-checking against the primary sources:
- **EUFEST:** 72% discontinuation was haloperidol only.
- **CATIE:** the intolerability figures are swapped.
- **Carbon 2018:** the TD figures are misreported.
- **Jauhar 2014:** the effect sizes are misreported.
- **Khan 2013:** three different references are cited for the same claim.
- **Wunderink 2013:** the figures are recovery rates, not functional remission.

The human-rights framing in Part I is the best-supported part. The "Iatrogenic Loop Theorem"
multiplies odds ratios for different outcomes and should not be called a theorem.

## 7. Toolkit tests that could actually fail

To replace the tests that cannot fail (audit list, §5 item 13). **All six are now in the toolkit**
(`toolkit/tests/test_falsifiable.py`; item 1 in `test_theorems.py`). Suite: 44 passed.

1. **Theorem 19 additivity through ∞ on 10⁴ random pairs.** Added in this commit. It failed on
   the old code and passes now.
2. **Proposition 3 by quadrature.** Compute X(m) = Var from random reference measures. Assert
   Var ≤ L(s_max − m). Fit the local exponent and compare it with 1+1/(a+2) to 0.02.
3. **Proposition 9a by integration.** Integrate ė = (1−e)^γ with event detection. Arrival
   must occur for γ < 1 and match `arrival_time`; no arrival may occur for γ ≥ 1.
4. **Theorem 26 on 10⁴ random pairs.** |gyration angle| must equal the area of (0, a, a⊕b).
5. **Theorems 20–22 by simulation.** A Milstein simulation of p must match the transformed
   rapidity simulation in distribution. The median must equal the logistic of the mean
   rapidity.
6. **Cross-checks.** `charts.hamacher_*` must equal `law.dial_*`, and `invariance_i2` must
   reproduce the committed H2 row for BCG.

## 8. What this programme can and cannot settle

A bounded observer cannot see everything. That is the premise, and it binds this review too.
What the programme can do is say precisely what a finite observer can know, keep and combine,
and test, system by system, whether real quantities meet those conditions. That is a real
contribution if the tests start winning. It will not, by itself, "solve reality". No single
law does that, and the programme's own discipline forbids claiming it. The fastest route to a
result that matters is narrow:

1. power-checked tests;
2. near the bound;
3. of the conditions rather than the law;
4. with one flagship, run to completion.
