# Inside: ten real cases, with a bounded retrospective view

The fictional river story in *Inside* asks what changes when an observer receives evidence that was unavailable from their position. This epilogue applies that question to ten documented incidents. Each scene begins with what a person or institution could observe at the time, then separates the interpretation they made from what surviving records support. The pullback is an evidence-based reconstruction. It is not an alien, all-seeing account of motives.

The gallery should let visitors feel the force of an incomplete view without ridiculing the people inside it. Some mistakes were understandable under limited access; some involved preventable procedure failures or decisions with grave consequences. Seeing why an inference was made does not erase responsibility or make every account equally supported. A surviving record can also remain incomplete.

The framework's finite-access premise and predictive-state criterion can motivate the *question* of what information an account omits. They do **not** mathematically prove that historical actors shared the same perceptions, that exchanging evidence will end conflict, or that a particular political or ethical conclusion follows. A ten-case gallery can demonstrate recurring forms of missing context; it cannot establish a universal law of social reconciliation.

`site/inside-cases-data.mjs` supplies the public scenes. In this ledger, `[P]` means a fact stated in a cited primary record, official inquiry, original recording, or research paper checked for this page. `[D]` marks our synthesis of those records. Neither tag promotes a historical example into a proof of the framework's social claims.

## 1. B-59 beneath the surface · 1962

- [P] The US procedure called for small explosive signals to tell a submerged Soviet submarine to surface. The National Security Archive presents the [US instruction and related documents](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB399/). Its account says the procedure reached the Soviet Foreign Ministry but submarine commanders later said they did not receive it.
- [P] The [Soviet Northern Fleet report](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB399/docs/Report%20of%20the%20submarine%20mission.pdf) records one nuclear torpedo on each boat and says underwater acoustic blasts could not be distinguished from depth bombs by the submarines.
- [D] The visual reversal is surface intent versus underwater evidence, with the nuclear payload invisible to the US crews. The Archive's [assessment of later testimony](https://nsarchive2.gwu.edu/NSAEBB/NSAEBB75/) calls the account of an order to ready the torpedo controversial. Do not claim a launch was imminent or one person certainly “saved the world.”

## 2. The green rocket at Dogger Bank · 1904

- [P] The [1905 International Commission of Inquiry](https://www.worldcourts.com/ici/eng/decisions/1905.02.26_doggerbank.htm) says the green rocket was a trawling signal. It also records earlier threat warnings, a wireless report from a Russian support ship claiming attack, and lookouts' later inference of a torpedo boat from a night silhouette.
- [P] The commission found no hostile act by the fishing vessels. Its majority found no torpedo boats near them, held the opening of fire unjustified, and recorded two fishermen killed and six wounded.
- [D] The first suspicious silhouette could not be firmly identified; the inquiry discussed possible Russian fire toward one of the fleet's own ships. Show the fatal decision and the evidence limits together. The [Permanent Court of Arbitration case record](https://pca-cpa.org/en/cases/317/) confirms the inquiry's institutional context.

## 3. The reported second attack · 1964

- [P] The [US National Archives](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/tonkin-gulf-resolution) distinguishes the real August 2 attack from the reported August 4 attack. It preserves the destroyer captain's subsequent doubts about weather effects and sonar, and records the resolution that authorized expanded US involvement in Vietnam.
- [P] An [NSA review](https://www.nsa.gov/portals/75/documents/news-features/declassified-documents/gulf-of-tonkin/articles/release-2/rel2_thoughts_intelligence.pdf) accepts that the August 4 attack did not occur while disputing a separate claim that NSA itself deliberately withheld intelligence.
- [D] The second view is the captain's doubt after the initial attack report. The later NSA reanalysis belongs in the retrospective reveal, not inside the contemporaneous scene: most NSA analysts initially believed an attack had occurred. The case is a chain from ambiguous instrument readings and eyewitness reports into a state narrative and authorization. It must never be shown as “no attack ever happened” or as proof that all participants had identical evidence or motives.

## 4. Two aircraft in the fog · 1977

- [P] The [Spanish accident investigation](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/2022-11/Spanish_AccReport.pdf), hosted by the FAA, found that the KLM captain began takeoff without clearance while Pan Am remained on the runway. The [FAA reconstruction](https://www.faa.gov/lessons_learned/transport_airplane/accidents/PH-BUF) details fog, radio wording, and overlapping transmissions. The collision killed 583 people.
- [D] The overhead runway view can reveal what no cockpit could see in time. The report places fundamental responsibility on the unauthorized KLM takeoff. Do not present this as equal fault or as a generic misunderstanding detached from the deaths.

## 5. The pump and the map · 1854

- [P] In [his own 1855 account](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/72894/72894-h/72894-h.htm), John Snow joined case locations with household water histories and implicated the Broad Street pump. The [CDC history](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/mm5334a1.htm) records the then-unpopular water explanation against the prevailing bad-air account and the removal of the handle on September 8.
- [P] Snow wrote that new cases had already declined before the handle was removed. The agent causing cholera was identified later; the pump episode did not by itself settle the microbiology.
- [D] The CDC's short historical summary says removing the handle prevented additional deaths; Snow's nearer account says the decline was already underway and he could not establish whether the well was still infectious. This exhibit follows Snow's narrower causal claim for the handle itself.
- [D] The reveal is a shared water source beneath households that seemed separate on a street map. Do not animate the handle removal as a single action that conclusively ended the outbreak. This is stronger as an evidence-model change than as two communities blaming each other.

## 6. The panic about the panic · 1938

- [P] The [Library of Congress account](https://blogs.loc.gov/loc/2016/10/lcm-page-from-the-past-war-of-the-worlds/) identifies the *War of the Worlds* radio show as a drama presented with news-bulletin conventions. It traces later sensational newspaper coverage that popularized a national-panic legend.
- [P] The [National Archives examination of responses](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2003/fall/war-of-worlds.html) contains both frightened listeners and people who recognized or checked the fiction.
- [D] There are two filters: some listeners took dramatic bulletins as news, then some reporting inflated the scale of that reaction. The precise size of the panic is contested. Do not replace “everyone panicked” with “nobody panicked.”

## 7. The grid crosses the border · 2003

- [P] The [joint US-Canada task force report](https://www.energy.gov/oe/downloads/blackout-2003-final-report-august-14-2003-blackout-united-states-and-canada-causes-and) traces the initiating events to northern Ohio and identifies interacting failures in planning, operator awareness, vegetation management, and regional diagnostics. The [Government of Canada release](https://www.canada.ca/en/news/archive/2004/04/canada-task-force-presents-final-report-blackout-august-2003.html) explains that both governments jointly issued the findings.
- [D] [Contemporaneous reporting of officials' statements](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/2003/08/17/after-trading-blame-us-canada-plan-a-joint-probe/89954b75-1834-4705-9516-fb50780e0090/) records early cross-border blame and a lightning explanation; the scene summarizes that reporting rather than a direct government transcript.
- [D] The zoom-out should expose one coupled electrical network, not a border-shaped cause. The outage had multiple documented contributing failures; no single group or fallen tree is a complete account. Early statements were provisional, not a settled national consensus.

## 8. The missing end of the speech · 2010

- [P] The [full NAACP recording of Shirley Sherrod's speech](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E9NcCa_KjXk) carries her story past the circulated excerpt to her help for the farmer and her account of what she learned. The experience she describes predated her USDA appointment.
- [P] At the [July 21 White House briefing](https://obamawhitehouse.archives.gov/photos-and-video/video/press-briefing-20100721), the government acknowledged that people acted without all the facts and said Agriculture Secretary Tom Vilsack would apologize. [Pew's contemporaneous media chronology](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2010/07/26/shirley-sherrod-page/) records how the short clip spread, her resignation followed, and the fuller account became public.
- [D] Here the edit removed the speech's reversal. The two accounts do not carry equal evidential weight; the early accusation and employment decision caused harm. Replaying the full recording can correct a particular accusation without resolving every political dispute surrounding it.

## 9. One photograph, different colors · 2015

- [P] A [PLOS ONE study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0161090) records the original disagreement and identifies the physical dress as blue and black. [Journal of Vision experiments](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28146253/) support different implicit assumptions about illumination as one explanation for the divergent appearances.
- [P] A further [vision study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5812438/) says the full set of mechanisms remains uncertain.
- [D] The observer really may perceive white and gold in the photograph. That is a fact about appearance, while blue and black describes the garment. The same photographic evidence does not entitle anyone to infer that the other viewer is dishonest or that illumination alone explains every individual response.

## 10. The warning that was a test · 2018

- [P] The [FCC investigation](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-350119A1.pdf) says an internal Hawaiʻi drill recording mixed exercise language with “this is not a drill”; a live statewide alert was sent at about 8:07 a.m. and a corrective alert followed 38 minutes later. The [State of Hawaiʻi investigation](https://dod.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/report2018-01-29-181149.pdf) documents procedural and supervisory failures.
- [P] The [state's day-of notice](https://dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/hawaii-emergency-management-agency-statement-on-missile-launch-false-alarm-january-13-2018/) confirmed no missile launch. The sending officer later claimed to have believed the threat was real; the FCC could not interview that officer and could not fully test that claim.
- [D] The visual reversal is the public's live alert beside the control room's intended exercise. A confusing script, weak safeguards, and slow correction mattered along with one person's action. Do not reduce the incident to a single wrong click.

## Editorial rules for the scene builder

1. Label **seen or heard**, **inferred then**, and **supported later** separately. Never write invented first-person thoughts or quotations for historical people.
2. Keep unequal responsibility visible. Tenerife, Dogger Bank, Tonkin, and Sherrod are particularly vulnerable to false equivalence if every panel receives the same moral weight.
3. Show unresolved evidence as unresolved. The B-59 command dialogue, the exact first Dogger Bank silhouette, the pump handle's effect, and the full mechanism of *The Dress* are not settled by a compelling animation.
4. Link each case to the sources beside the retrospective claim. If a scene needs a detail unsupported by those sources, remove the detail or obtain another source before shipping it.
5. Present this gallery as an invitation to revise an account with evidence. A shared reconstruction may improve understanding; it does not guarantee agreement, forgiveness, or a common future.
